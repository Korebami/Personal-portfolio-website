Monopropellant Engine for Untethered Soft Robotics

A summer at MIT's Therapeutic Technology Design & Development Lab — modeling, building, and testing a pressure-amplifying actuator engine from the bench up.

Overview

Predicting — then proving — actuator behavior

This project developed a MATLAB-based predictive model to characterize and optimize the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide in propellant-based actuators for untethered soft robotics, focusing on critical parameters: chamber geometry, catalyst size, reaction rate, temperature, and pressure.

A key conceptual contribution was introducing residence time as a design variable — establishing its importance for fuel efficiency and control. Findings showed that reducing the reactor's width-to-height ratio enhances residence time, improving both predictability and power output.

In the Lab

From simulation to bench testing

Experimental validation paired every model prediction with bench testing — adjusting fuel volume, flow rate, and catalyst surface area to assess real pressurization behavior.

Engineering Outcome

A full-seal redesign

Beyond modeling, the fuel reactor engine itself was redesigned to achieve a full seal — eliminating leaks and improving retention by 50%, enabling reliable integration into full robotic systems.

01

Width-to-height ratio reduction increased residence time, directly improving predictability and power.

02

Full-seal redesign improved fuel retention by 50%, removing a key failure mode for untethered operation.

Mentorship

Paying it forward

Alongside the core research, this summer included mentoring a high school student through a parallel project — guiding their analysis of fuel injection methods and fostering their understanding of energy-efficient design in soft robotics.

This experience directly led to a continued role as an MSRP Ambassador and application reviewer from December 2025 to March 2026.
